# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We pledge to make participation in the OpenData community a harassment-free and
welcoming experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, disability,
ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of
experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance,
race, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

## Expected behaviour

Examples of behaviour that contributes to a positive community include:

- showing empathy and respect for different viewpoints and experiences;
- giving and accepting constructive feedback gracefully;
- focusing discussion on what is best for the project and its users;
- accepting responsibility, apologising when appropriate and learning from
  mistakes;
- respecting privacy and avoiding the disclosure of personal information.

Unacceptable behaviour includes harassment, intimidation, discriminatory or
sexualised language, personal attacks, trolling, deliberate disruption,
publishing private information, or other conduct that could reasonably make
participation unsafe or unwelcome.

## Scope

This code applies in project spaces and when an individual officially represents
the project in public spaces. Project spaces include repositories, issue
trackers, pull requests, discussions, email and project events.

## Reporting and enforcement

Report conduct concerns privately to `terry.curran@towermarsh.co.uk`. Reports
will be reviewed promptly and handled as confidentially as practical. Anyone
reviewing a report must avoid conflicts of interest and protect the privacy and
safety of affected participants.

Project maintainers may remove, edit or reject comments, commits, code, issues
and other contributions that conflict with this code. Responses may include a
private warning, temporary participation restrictions, suspension or a
permanent ban, depending on severity and pattern of behaviour.

Retaliation against a person who makes a good-faith report is itself a violation.
Knowingly false or malicious reports may also result in enforcement action.
